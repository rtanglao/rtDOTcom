>If you experience rejection sensitive dysphoria, one piece of bad news can feel like confirmation of everything you have ever feared, and you feel it in your body before you can think about it. That is not a character flaw. It is information about how your nervous system processes threat. Naming it does not make it disappear, but it creates enough distance to take a breath.

>So the practice is three steps. Catch it. Name it. Revise it.

>Catching it means noticing the draft and saying, out loud or on paper, this is my stormy first draft. You do not have to believe a sentence just because it is loud.

>Naming it means asking what kind of sentence it is: a time frame problem, an identity collapse, a verdict where there should be data.

>Revising it usually takes one word. That word is often yet.

>"I’m bad at networking” is a closed door. “I’m not good at networking yet” is a different sentence entirely. So is “I’m currently not good at networking,” or “I’m learning to network,” or “I’m building my networking skills,” or “for me, networking is unfamiliar.”

>Two other phrases can do a lot of work: “I haven’t figured out how to sell in a way that feels like me yet,” and “what would it take?”
